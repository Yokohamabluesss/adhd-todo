# Why a task board for ADHD?

[中文版](#zh)

Imagine spending ten minutes choosing between a lesson and an outline, then finishing the outline and wondering if you should squeeze in the lesson too. ADHD Todo offers a way to hand a little of each decision to chance. A draw can pick the first small action. A Bingo line can be the stopping cue you agreed to before you began.

## Where a round can help

| Where someone gets stuck | A choice the board offers | What to watch for in a playtest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seeing the whole backlog makes opening it feel like work. | Leave ideas in the two libraries. Put only the tasks you want to face now on the board. A round with two tasks is legitimate. | Can someone start a small round while the rest of their list stays in the library? |
| Several tasks seem equally reasonable. | Draw one square. If the suggestion makes you realize you would rather do something else, select that square yourself. | After seeing the suggestion, can the person name a first action, including one they chose themselves? |
| An unfinished list makes it hard to stop, even after doing enough for today. | Before starting, choose a lucky line as permission to wrap up. Remaining squares carry over when you end the round. | When Bingo appears, does the player feel free to close the board? |
| Two projects both matter and neither fits neatly at the top of a list. | Put a small action from each on the board. Draw again or switch projects by choosing a square. | Can the player return to the first project without rebuilding a list? |
| A large task has a clear deadline but no obvious first move. | Open **Task steps** under that square and write one small action, such as “Find the assignment brief”. | Can the person start that action before they have worked out every later step? |
| Attention shifts halfway through. | Keep the square in place. Leave a note about the exact place to resume and carry unfinished steps into the next round. | Coming back tomorrow, can the person pick up without reconstructing yesterday’s plan? |
| Starting seems to mean agreeing to finish everything. | A completed line and all Must-do tasks earn a successful round. Clearing a sparse board also succeeds. The player chooses when to end. | When a line lights up, can the person decide for themselves whether today’s work is enough? |
| The plan changes, or an unfinished evening feels like a lost day. | In Set up, drag an unfinished task back to its own library. Work already finished stays visible on the activity calendar, even if the round ended early. | Can someone set aside an unrealistic task and still find what they did finish? |

## Give a project more chances to come up

Put what is on your mind on the board. If that is “Write my paper”, save it once in the Everyday library and place three copies with that same name. Each eligible copy gets a chance in the draw. When one comes up, do as much writing as suits this turn.

### When a task has steps inside it

“Send the outline” can occupy one square even if it takes several sittings. Inside it, you might write “Find the brief”, “Draft three headings” and “Email the file”. Finish the first step and its square shows a check. If you switch to reading halfway through drafting, a note such as “Heading two needs an example” tells you more on your return than “in progress”. A photo of the relevant page can serve the same purpose.

The one-layer step grid keeps those decisions inside the task. Someone glancing at the main board still sees one outline. They can add steps as they discover them, do them in any order, and decide when the outline has actually been sent. Only confirming the parent square awards points and counts toward Bingo and the completion log. Someone who needs six steps to send the same outline gets the same one completed deliverable as someone who needs two.

## Why the other rules are there

| Design choice | What it lets the player do |
| --- | --- |
| Everyday and one-off libraries | Keep “Read a lesson” available for repeated use. Place two copies and mark just tomorrow’s assignment Must-do. A one-off such as “Send the outline” moves onto the board and can return to the library with its steps and note if plans change. |
| Must-do and weighted draws | Give an urgent task first chance without giving up the right to pick something else. With one Standard square and one Low square, the default odds are two in three and one in three. The animation reveals the choice, and Guide shows the odds for the actual board. |
| Points and shuffle | Spend earned points to rearrange the board. Completed marks move too, so a line that looked distant can come within reach. Shuffling costs one point per square completed this round. Row grouping keeps, for example, study tasks in their study row. |
| Empty squares and manual endings | An empty square earns no part of a line. A player who places two tasks and finishes both can still close a successful round. If a line appears while a Must-do remains, ending the round brings up a reminder. Unfinished work stays in place for next time, and the player chooses what to put in the gaps. |
| Separate boards, day boundaries and saves | Keep a study routine apart from household work. Someone who often studies after midnight can choose when their board day begins. Closing the window saves your place. A SQLite export carries an open round to the web edition or another Windows computer. Clearing the grid, resetting today and resetting all progress are separate choices. |
| Optional Stats and a sample board | Try a round on the sample and explore its labeled simulated history. On a personal board, compare first suggestions with the tasks actually completed. If suggestions are often passed over, try hand-picking the first task for a few rounds and see how starting feels. The question marks offer more ways to read the figures. [Metrics and examples](metrics.md) |

A useful question after a trial is “What did you actually do after opening the board?” Writing a heading, switching to a better task or deliberately stopping can each be the right outcome. The player’s account helps explain what a completion count alone cannot tell us.

# 为什么给 ADHD 做一块任务棋盘？

假设你花了十分钟才在读课文和写提纲之间选定一件，写完提纲又想，是不是还该挤时间读一课？ADHD Todo 想帮上这两个时刻。抽一格，让运气替你选第一步。开局前也可以给自己一个约定，等碰巧连成一线，今天就安心收工。

## 一轮里，哪些地方容易卡住？

| 使用时的卡点 | 棋盘给了什么选择 | 试玩时值得观察什么 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 一打开待办就要面对所有积压，光看已经很累。 | 想法先放在两个任务库里，眼下愿意碰的几件事才上板。只摆两项也可以开一轮。 | 其余事项还在库里时，能不能放心开始这两项？ |
| 几件事都有理由先做，越比越难选。 | 请棋盘抽一格。看见结果才发现自己其实想做另一件，就直接点那件。 | 看见推荐后，能不能说出接下来要做的第一个动作？自己改选也算。 |
| 已经做了不少，看着剩下的任务还是觉得不该停。 | 开局前就和自己约好，碰上 Bingo 可以收工。手动结束后，未完成的格子留到下一轮。 | 看到一条线亮起时，能不能安心关掉棋盘？ |
| 两个项目都想推进，给它们排先后反而费劲。 | 每个项目各摆一个小动作。做完可以再抽，也可以自己换一格。 | 换过项目之后，能不能直接回来接着做？ |
| 知道明天要交什么，却不知道现在从哪下手。 | 打开该格的「任务步骤」，先写“找到作业要求”这样一个小动作。 | 后面几步还没想全，能不能已经开始做第一步？ |
| 做到一半转去做别的，回来忘了断在哪里。 | 格子留在原处。把回来的落脚点写进备注，没做完的步骤也能带到下一轮。 | 明天回来，是否还得重新推一遍昨天的思路？ |
| 一想到开始就意味着整张清单都要做完，干脆先不开始。 | 完成一条线并做完所有必做任务，本轮就算成功。少摆几项并全部做完也算。什么时候收工由自己定。 | 一条线亮起来时，是不是看得出今天可以停在这里，也可以继续？ |
| 临时改了计划，晚上只记得那些没做成的事。 | 在摆盘页把暂时做不了的任务拖回原来的任务库。即使提前收工，活动日历也会留下当天真正完成的项数。 | 推迟一项不现实的安排后，是否还能说出今天确实做成了什么？ |

## 想多做哪件事，就给它多几个机会

把眼前惦记的事放上来就好。比如今天更想写论文，就在日常库只存一份「写论文」，在主棋盘放三个同名格。每个符合抽选条件的格子都有自己的机会。抽到时，这一回合想写多少就写多少。

### 也可以给一项任务留几个内部步骤

“交提纲”可能要分几次坐下来才能做完。打开这一格，可以写下“找到作业要求”“列三个小标题”“发出文件”。做完第一步，子格原位留下一个勾。如果写到一半突然转去读书，一句“第二个小标题还缺例子”，比只留一个“进行中”更能帮明天的自己接上思路。需要对照教材时，也可以把那一页拍下来放进备注。

步骤矩阵只拆一层，主板上始终看得到完整的那件事。想到下一步再补，可以跳着做，也可以自己判断提纲什么时候真的交出去了。只有手动完成主格，才发点、记一次完成并参与 Bingo。同一份提纲，有人需要拆六步，有人只需两步，最后都只交了一份。这样，拆得多细可以完全服从当下的需要。

## 其他规则在实际使用中有什么用？

| 设计选择 | 放到一轮里有什么用 |
| --- | --- |
| 日常库和临时库 | “读一节课”可以反复上板。今天读两节，可以只把明天要交作业的那一节设成「必做」。像“交提纲”这样的临时任务只移动一份，改期时连同步骤和备注一起退回库里。 |
| 必做优先、其余按权重抽选 | 想让急事先被推荐，又想保留临时改选的自由，就把急事设为「必做」。没有必做格时，若盘上只有一个标准格和一个低优先级格，默认机会分别是三分之二和三分之一。动画揭晓结果，「使用指南」里能看当前棋盘的实际概率。 |
| 点数和打乱 | 做任务得到的点数可以换一次新布局，完成标记也跟着移动，有时就离一条线近了。本轮已完成几格，打乱就花几点。若想让学习任务始终好找，可以按行分组，只在行内打乱。 |
| 空格和手动收工 | 一行里的空白格不能帮你凑 Bingo；但全盘只放两项，两项都做完也能成功。连线时若还有必做任务，点结束会再提醒一次。没做完的留在原处，下一轮空出来的位置由你决定要不要补、补什么。 |
| 独立棋盘、分日和存档 | 学习和家务可以分开摆，也分开看记录。经常过了午夜才学习，可以自己定几点算新一天。关窗休息后可以接着做。换电脑或换到网页版时，可以用 SQLite 存档带走正在做的一轮。想重新摆盘、只重置今天或清除所有进度，也各有对应的选项。 |
| 按需查看的统计和示例棋盘 | 初次打开可以先试玩，再看明确标注为模拟的记录。用自己的棋盘一段时间后，可以看看首抽推荐和实际完成的是不是同一项。如果经常改选，下几轮试着自己挑第一项，观察哪种方式更容易动手。指标旁的问号提供解读方向。[统计口径和例子](metrics.md#zh) |

试玩后值得问一句：“打开棋盘以后，你接下来真的做了什么？”写下一个小标题、换去做更合适的任务，或者觉得今天已经够了，都可能是合适的结果。完成数能记下做了几项，这个决定为什么对自己有用，还得听使用者怎么说。
